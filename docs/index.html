painTest

A face photo in, an estimated pain score out. Built so wrinkles on older faces are less likely to get counted as pain.

Source on GitHub How to run it

What it is

You take a picture of a face on iPhone. A Mac running OpenFace measures facial Action Units. The app turns those into estimated PSPI (0 to 16), the same kind of scale researchers use. You can add your own 0 to 10 rating, save history, look at a graph, and export a CSV.

It is not a doctor and not a diagnosis. It can still act as a personal pain log: a face-based estimate plus your own rating, so patterns show up that you would miss from memory alone.

Why it exists

Facial pain software is often trained on younger faces. Wrinkles can look like a frown or a squint, so older people get worse scores. Most apps just run the photo as-is. painTest lets you type your real age and optionally de-age toward a younger target (around 25) before OpenFace runs, then it keeps the original score next to it.

What you can do

  • Guided camera with a face oval and live yaw / quality hints
  • Review, retake, or reuse the last good capture
  • Estimated PSPI and a full Action Unit list
  • Self-report 0 to 10 and notes
  • History, trends, CSV export
  • Same-Wi-Fi auto-find of the Mac, or a tunnel when you are away

Limits

OpenFace runs on your Mac, not on Apple’s servers. Photos stay on the phone and that computer. Bad lighting and a turned head still wreck the number, which is why low-confidence shots get flagged. Treat the score as an estimate you track, not as a reason to start or stop treatment.
